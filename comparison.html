🇵🇭 Philippines Food Price — Multi-Model Forecast Dashboard

Five ML models (each tuned across 5 hyperparameter variants) trained on WFP historical price data (2000–2023), validated against 2024–Jan 2026 actuals, and forecasted through Dec 2027. The best-performing variant of each model is used for all charts below.

📦 Data: WFP Humanitarian Data Exchange 🗓 Train: 2000–2023 ✅ Validation: 2024–Jan 2026 🔮 Forecast: Feb 2026–Dec 2027 🛒 73 commodities 🗺 17 regions

📐 Methodology: Hyperparameter Search & Model Selection

For each of the 5 model types, 5 hyperparameter variants were trained on data up to 2023, then evaluated on held-out 2024–Jan 2026 actual prices. The variant with the lowest validation MAPE per commodity was retained. This ensures each model family is represented by its absolute best configuration rather than an arbitrary default.

  • Features used: Year number, sine/cosine month encoding (seasonality), lagged prices (1, 3, 6, 12 months), rolling means (3, 6, 12 months), first and 12-month price differences, region and price-type label encoding.
  • Validation metric: Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) on held-out 2024+ data.
  • Forecast method: Recursive/autoregressive — each predicted month becomes a feature (lag/MA) for the next month, allowing the model to forecast into 2027.
  • Scale: Ridge & KNN receive StandardScaler-normalised features; tree-based models use raw values.

1. Historical Price Trend + Model Predictions

What this shows: Actual monthly prices from WFP field surveys (white line) alongside each model's predictions during the validation period (2024–Jan 2026, coloured dashed lines). Prices are averaged across all markets in the selected region group. Use the dropdowns to explore any commodity, price type, and whether to show all models or just one. A model that closely follows the white line during validation is generalising well to unseen data. Gaps between a model line and the actual line reveal systematic over- or under-prediction bias for that commodity.




2. Prediction Error Distribution

What this shows: A histogram of percentage prediction errors (predicted − actual) / actual × 100 for the selected model across all validation-period data points. Bars to the right of zero mean the model over-predicted (higher than actual); bars to the left mean it under-predicted. A tight, symmetric distribution centred near 0% is ideal — it means the model is both accurate and unbiased. A skewed distribution reveals a systematic bias that may be correctable with post-processing.


3. Model Performance Comparison (Radar)

What this shows: Each axis represents a different accuracy dimension. A model whose polygon fills more of the chart is better overall. Axes are inverted so that smaller MAPE / MAE appears on the outside (better). R² is plotted directly (higher = better). This makes it easy to spot if a model is strong on one metric but weak on another — for example, low MAPE but high bias. Use the commodity dropdown to see how the ranking changes for a specific food item.


4. Future Price Forecast (Feb 2026 – Dec 2027)

What this shows: Projected prices generated by each model from Feb 2026 through Dec 2027 using a recursive (autoregressive) forecasting strategy — each monthly prediction is fed back as a lagged feature for the next month. The white line shows the most recent actual prices for context. Solid coloured lines show each model's forecast; select a single model to focus. The spread between model lines represents forecast uncertainty — where models agree, confidence is higher. Diverging lines in late 2027 indicate growing uncertainty as the models extrapolate further from their training data. Commodities with strong seasonal cycles (e.g. vegetables) will show repeating wave patterns.




5. Per-Commodity Model Accuracy Table

What this shows: Validation-period MAPE (%) and MAE (PHP) for every commodity, split across all five models. Grade badges summarise each cell: A ≤5% excellent, B 5–10% good, C 10–20% fair, D >20% poor. Click any column header to sort. Use the filter tabs to focus on a specific grade tier or model. The "n" column shows how many validation data points were used for that commodity — smaller samples can inflate MAPE even for a good model. A commodity with grade D across all models may have structural price shocks (policy interventions, supply disruptions) that are difficult to forecast from historical patterns alone.




6. Accuracy Grade Distribution

What this shows: The proportion of commodities that fall into each accuracy grade for the selected model. A larger green A-slice means more commodities are predicted within 5% of actual prices. Use the dropdown to compare how grade distributions shift across models — tree-based models typically have more A-grade commodities than linear models on this non-linear dataset. The grade counts next to each label also serve as a quick quality scorecard.


7. MAPE by Commodity — Ranked

What this shows: Bar chart of MAPE values for each commodity, sorted from lowest to highest error. Green bars are A-grade, yellow B, orange C, red D. Use the model dropdown to see how each model's accuracy profile differs across commodities. Models that perform well on some commodities (e.g. rice — stable, well-surveyed) may struggle with others (e.g. fresh vegetables — high volatility, sparse data). The vertical dashed lines mark the grade thresholds at 5%, 10% and 20%.


8. Hyperparameter Search — 5 Variants per Model

What this shows: For each model family, five different hyperparameter configurations were trained on the 2000–2023 dataset and evaluated on the 2024–Jan 2026 validation period. The configuration with the lowest validation MAPE was selected as the "best" and is used for all predictions shown in this dashboard. The ★ row highlights the winning configuration. Testing multiple variants guards against sub-optimal defaults and ensures each model is represented at near its full potential capability.
