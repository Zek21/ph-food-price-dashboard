Philippines Food Price Forecast Dashboard

Gradient Boosting model trained on WFP actual prices (2000 – Jan 2026), validated against 15,356 data points, now forecasting through Dec 2027. Built from 73 commodities across 17 Philippine regions.

📊 About This Model — Gradient Boosting Regressor

This dashboard uses a Gradient Boosting regression model trained on 143,000+ actual WFP (World Food Programme) price records spanning 2000 to January 2026. The model uses time-series features to capture commodity price dynamics:

  • Lag features: Previous 1, 3, 6, and 12-month prices for the same commodity & region
  • Rolling statistics: 3-month and 6-month rolling averages and standard deviations
  • Seasonality: Month-of-year encoding to capture seasonal fluctuations (e.g., harvest periods)
  • Trend: Year feature to capture long-term inflation and price trends
  • Categorical encoding: Region, commodity, and price type encoded as numeric features

The model was validated on 15,356 actual data points from 2024 – Jan 2026 that were held out from training. Forecasts extend through December 2027 using iterative prediction (each month's prediction becomes input for the next month).

⚠️ What Was the Old Model? (Removed)

The previous model was a Random Forest regressor trained on the original Dataset WFP2.csv (153K rows, 2000–2023). It generated 22.6 million predicted price records stored in merged_file_with_location_2.csv. That model has been removed from this dashboard due to the following critical problems:

1. No time-series awareness: The Random Forest used only raw categorical features (commodity name, region, year, month) without lag prices, rolling averages, or trend indicators. It couldn't learn how prices evolve over time — it essentially guessed based on historical averages, making it unable to follow real-world price momentum.

2. Data duplication & scale mixing: The prediction output contained multiple rows per (commodity, region, month) at different aggregation levels (marked by different "Priceflag" values: actual vs. aggregate). For example, Eggs in Region IV-A for January 2024 had 126 duplicate rows with prices ranging from PHP 7 to PHP 113, mixing per-unit and per-kilogram scales, inflating averages by up to 750%.

3. No post-2023 training data: The model was trained only through 2023 and had to blindly extrapolate into 2024–2027 without seeing actual price trends, producing unrealistic flat projections or wild spikes for many commodities.

2. Prediction Error Distribution

How far off are the model's predictions from actual prices?

This histogram shows the percentage error for every validation data point where we had both a prediction and an actual price (2024 – Jan 2026). Each bar represents a range of errors. The error is calculated as: (predicted − actual) ÷ actual × 100%.

How to read it: A perfect model would have all bars stacked at 0%. Bars to the left of zero mean the model under-predicted (real price was higher than forecast). Bars to the right mean it over-predicted (forecast was too high). Green bars are within ±5% error (excellent), yellow within ±10% (acceptable), and red beyond ±10% (poor). The strong clustering near zero shows the model rarely misses by more than 10%.

3. Per-Commodity Accuracy

Model accuracy graded for each of the 59 validated commodities

This table lists every commodity that had enough actual price records in 2024–Jan 2026 to evaluate the model. Two key metrics are shown: MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error) — the average absolute % miss, and MAE (Mean Absolute Error) — the average miss in Philippine Pesos.

Grading scale: Grade A = MAPE ≤ 5% (excellent — predictions nearly match reality), Grade B = 5–10% (good — minor deviations), Grade C = 10–20% (moderate — noticeable gaps), Grade D = >20% (poor — predictions unreliable).

"Validation Pts" shows how many actual price records were available for testing — more points means higher confidence in the grade. Click any column header to sort. Use the grade filter buttons to focus on specific accuracy tiers.

4. Future Price Forecast: Feb 2026 — Dec 2027

Predicted monthly prices from the Gradient Boosting model, with recent actuals for context

This chart focuses on the forward-looking prediction window. The solid white line shows recent actual prices (from 2023 onward) to give you a sense of where prices have been heading. The green dashed line extends into the future with the model's month-by-month forecasts.

How forecasts are generated: The model predicts one month at a time. Each month's predicted price becomes part of the input (as a lag feature) to predict the next month. This iterative forecasting means uncertainty grows the further out we predict — December 2027 forecasts are inherently less certain than March 2026.

What to look for: Seasonal patterns (fish and vegetable prices often dip around harvest months), gradual inflation trends, and whether the forecast continues the trajectory of actual recent prices smoothly.
